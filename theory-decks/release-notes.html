Final Release · Version I

Theory Decks · Final Release

Capabilities, design, FAQ, how-to, application, troubleshooting and a quick-reference cheat sheet. Version 1.0 history follows.

I

Final Release · DAW & Fretboard

The final release sets the DAW’s first-use grid to quarter notes and expands Fretboard from preset 2–8-string diagrams to a configurable 1–14-string instrument. Set each string’s pitch and octave, choose any displayed fret window from 0 through 48, and place a functional capo.

Capabilities

  • DAW: a fresh install and migrated previous default open with Grid = 1/4 (24 ticks). Explicit grid choices remain saved; a legacy saved 1/16 default is migrated once.
  • Fretboard strings: choose 1–14 strings. Standard, half-step down, whole-step down and drop presets populate the tuning; edit every string independently by note and octave.
  • Fret range: choose inclusive start and end frets from 0–48. The SVG shows that exact window and scrolls horizontally instead of compressing a wide neck.
  • Capo: choose fret 0–48. The capo bar is drawn on the neck; positions below it are muted/hidden, the capoed open pitch is shown beside each string, and tapped frets sound their concert pitch.
  • Shape catalog: 563 entries in 11 groups: 215 scales/collections, 224 chord sonorities and 124 intervals. This adds 117 spelled simple/compound intervals, 205 distinct rooted three-/four-note pitch sets and 200 unique five- through eight-tone synthetic collections. Formula labels are explicit; synthetic sets are not given invented traditional names. Search by group, name or formula.
  • Playback: ▶ Arpeggio plays the selected scale, chord or interval note-by-note in formula order; ■ Stop cancels remaining tones.
  • Exports: save the current diagram as PNG/JPG, a one-page PDF, a PowerPoint slide, or a Word document. Export selected formula playback as a Standard MIDI file with tempo, notes and tuning/capo metadata.
  • Saved locally: string count, every open pitch, tuning preset, fret window, capo, key, shape and labels persist on this device. Export generation and audio synthesis are local; no online library is loaded.

Design

The Fretboard controls separate instrument, fret window and capo. Search narrows the grouped catalog by category, name or formula before choosing a shape, keeping the 563-entry list manageable on touch screens. A preset is a starting point, not a lock: changing one string switches to Custom. Frets use physical positions; labels and audio remain at concert pitch. On phones, controls wrap and the neck pans horizontally.

How To

  1. Open Fretboard and choose the number of strings.
  2. Choose a tuning preset or expand “Tune each string”; set each row’s note and octave. The chosen open pitches are shown beside the strings.
  3. Enter Start fret and End fret (inclusive). Use 0 to include open strings; set a smaller range to zoom in or up to 48 for a higher register.
  4. Set Capo fret to 0 for none, or to the physical fret where it is clamped. The marker appears when that fret is inside the displayed range.
  5. Select a key, type a category, name or interval formula in Find shape, choose a grouped result and select Notes or Degrees. Tap a dot for its exact pitch; use ▶ Arpeggio for note-by-note playback, ■ Stop to halt it, or choose an export format and select Export.
  6. In the DAW, Grid now starts at 1/4. Choose another resolution any time; it is remembered on this device.

Application

  • Model extended-range, re-entrant, open, drop, half-step-down or other custom instruments by editing each string’s note and octave.
  • Use Start/End to isolate a position, map a scale shape or inspect a high-fret chord without shrinking the full neck.
  • Use Capo to see only playable notes at and above the clamp, compare the capoed open pitches, and practice shapes while reading concert-pitch names.
  • Use the DAW’s 1/4 grid for broad rhythmic placement, then move to eighths, sixteenths or triplets for detail.

FAQ

  • Is the end fret included? Yes. Both endpoints are included; Start 0 / End 15 displays open strings and frets 1–15.
  • Does capo change the note names? No. Labels are concert pitch. Capo marks the clamp, hides frets below it, and shows each string’s open pitch raised by the capo distance.
  • Can I set a different tuning on every string? Yes. Expand Tune each string and choose a note and octave for each of the selected 1–14 strings.
  • What does MIDI export contain? The selected formula in concert-pitch order at 120 BPM, one note per event; tuning, capo and formula are recorded as track metadata. It does not encode every repeated fretboard position.
  • Why are some entries called synthetic collections or chord sets? Their pitch sets are precisely defined by the displayed formula, but they do not have a single established traditional name; the app avoids assigning one inaccurately.
  • Why does the DAW still show my old grid? Deliberately saved resolutions are retained. The old implicit 1/16 default is migrated to 1/4 once; selecting a grid stores that choice.
  • Does Fretboard need internet? No. Its renderer, tunings, notes, audio and controls are local. Hosted offline use needs one successful cache/install pass; an extracted local copy runs from its files.

Troubleshooting

  • Capo marker not visible: set Start ≤ Capo ≤ End. A capo outside the window is reported below the diagram but does not change the selected fret range.
  • Some strings show no target notes: confirm the open pitch and octave, selected key/shape and fret window; edit the string in Tune each string.
  • Board is wider than the phone: swipe the board sideways; the fret cells retain a useful size rather than being squeezed.
  • No audio: tap Play or a note once to start audio, then check device volume. iPhone/Safari may require that first user gesture.
  • Image export is blank: allow this browser to create a local image/printable file and retry; the diagram is never sent to a server.
  • No search results: clear Find shape or try a formula token such as ♭3 or ♯4.
  • Controls show an old version offline: open Settings → Offline while online once, or replace the extracted app folder with the Final Release files.

Cheat Sheet

  • String pitch: MIDI note = selected open-string MIDI + physical fret number.
  • Capo: playable frets are at/above capo; capoed open pitch = open MIDI + capo fret. Names/audio are concert pitch.
  • Fret window: start and end are inclusive; 0 is the open-string position; max supported fret is 48.
  • Fretboard playback: selected formula notes play in written order; MIDI exports at 120 BPM with 480 ticks per quarter.
  • Fretboard exports: PNG/JPG image · PDF page · PPTX slide · DOCX page · MIDI arpeggio.
  • Fretboard catalog: curated named forms + formula-labeled collections; interval spelling and pitch-set formula define the structure.
  • DAW grid: 1/4 = 24 ticks · 1/8 = 12 · 1/16 = 6 · 1/32 = 3 (triplet choices remain available).
  • Tuning presets: Standard · Half step down · Whole step down · Drop (lowest string −2) · Custom.
NEW

50 Common Progression Game Decks

Fifty new game groups are available from Play → Games. Every group has exactly 30 locally generated progression cards: ten related harmonic forms, each in triad, seventh and extended-color voicings. Every deal also includes a key, a guitar technique and a Base 0 formula, using the existing shuffle bags, manual pickers, audio and card-flip controls.

Capabilities

  • 1,500 progression cards total across 50 independent game pools; every pool meets the 30-card minimum.
  • Progressions transpose to any selected major key; minor-family games use its relative minor.
  • Each deal has independent key, progression and technique pickers, plus random reshuffle and Deal again.
  • Every progression card flips for details, has Base 0 chord-root offsets, and plays together or arpeggiated at exact pitch.
  • The game pools are generated from local formulas and are included in the offline cache. No download, font, library or remote API is needed.

Design

The progression formula is the organizing idea. Each family keeps its recognizable cadence or bass movement while nearby substitutions create ten playable forms. Those forms are rendered as plain triads, seventh chords and extended colors. The games reuse the same touch-aware shuffle-bag engine as the existing games, so random deals avoid immediate repeats and any card can also be selected directly.

How To

  1. Open Play and choose a game from the Common chord progressions group, or open Choose game to see the complete linked catalog.
  2. Use the key selector to set the major key; minor families show its relative minor in the deal summary.
  3. Tap 🔀 above a key, progression or technique to replace only that item. Use its picker or ◀ / ▶ to choose a specific card.
  4. Tap a progression card to flip through the chord details and Base 0 formula. Use its playback buttons to hear block chords or an arpeggio.
  5. Choose Deal again for a fresh key, progression and technique together.

Application

  • Guitar practice: play the roots first, then close-position triads, inversions and the dealt technique through the form.
  • Composition: keep the bass contour of a family while choosing a different harmonic variation for the verse, chorus or bridge.
  • Ear training: listen before flipping, identify the cadence, then check the progression and voicing.
  • Improvisation: target each changing chord’s 3rd and 7th, connecting them with notes from the selected key.

FAQ

  • Why 30 cards per game? Ten progression forms are each represented as triad, seventh and extended-color voicings.
  • Are these 30 copies in different keys? No. They are ten harmonic variations with three voicing treatments; key is a separate, selectable deal slot.
  • Do these cards change Settings → Active cards? No. These are generated game-specific pools. The key and technique slots still use the existing active-card rules.
  • Can I use them without internet? Yes. Their generator, game engine, scripts and page dependencies are local and cached with the app.
  • Does “minor” mean a new minor key picker? The key picker remains the existing major-key deck. Minor families are played from the selected key’s relative minor.

Troubleshooting

  • No active cards: if the key or technique pool is switched off in Settings, turn cards back on there; the standard empty-pool message links to Settings.
  • No sound: tap a playback control once to allow the device audio context to start, check device volume and silent-mode behavior, then try ▶ Arpeggio.
  • Game link not opening: choose the entry from Play → Games; each game also has a direct play.html#game= link in the catalog.
  • Offline page missing: on a hosted install, open Settings → Offline and save all files once while online. A local copy opened from disk needs no service worker.
  • Old game list: refresh the page after the offline cache updates; this release increments the local cache version.

Theory Decks 1.0 is the first complete release: a guitar study tool that turns music theory and technique into flippable cards and dealt practice games — 150,038 cards in 21 decks and 131 games, every pitched card playable at exact pitch, plus a DAW that turns cards into full songs — running entirely on your device with no internet connection after the files are present locally.

  • 150,038 cards across 21 decks (Right Hand counted for every string set 2–8)
  • 131 games in 8 groups
  • 20 decks with pitch-perfect playback: ▶ Together / ▶ Chords, ▶ Arpeggio and the new + Octave switch
  • 50 colour themes and 20 card styles
  • DAW: Blank songs or 916 ready-made progressions in 20 genres, step grid to 32nd notes, per-bar meter, beat grouping, tempo, key, scale and chord changes, sections and song arrangement, live key / scale / chord changes

  • Settings: look & sound, active cards, DAW song, offline status, backup / restore / reset

Final update · 1.0 release

The final 1.0 update: a full review of the app and of the DAW, 100 new progressions, a complete Settings page and installable offline use. Everything below works with no internet connection.

  • 100 new DAW progressions in ten new genres — Film & game, Latin, R&B & neo-soul, Metal, Classical, Worship, Electronic, Hip-hop & lo-fi, Musical theatre, Celtic & folk — plus new Pop, Rock, Jazz, Blues, Gospel and Lydian entries. 47 duplicated progressions were removed, so all 916 are unique.

Opens in any DAW or notation program. What is exported follows the Play setting (the whole song, or this section), and the file is named Key - Song Title - bpm - Swing - grid - note - play, e.g. “E♭ Natural minor - Night Drive - 92 bpm - Swing Medium - Grid 1-16 - Note 1-8 - Song.mid”. Checked against an independent MIDI reader: tempo map, meters, key signatures and every note start match the DAW's own playback to within a millisecond.
  • Every known limitation addressed (see Known limitations): protected storage, the whole app as a .zip that runs with no connection ever, a background-proof DAW clock, double flats spelled correctly, a Drums lane, MIDI import, a quieter iOS fix and clearer low strings.
  • Play a card in the DAW: ▶ Together / ▶ Chords and ▶ Arpeggio for the selected card, in any key chosen in the card's Key box (key cards in their own key).
  • New song from a card: a progression becomes a song (bars per chord, repeats and key as chosen); a chord card four bars of that chord with its arpeggio as the melody; a Roman numeral card that chord alternating with the tonic; key and scale cards I–IV–V–I in that scale with the scale as the melody; pattern and interval cards I–IV–V–I with the card as the melody. Undo brings the old song back.
  • Find a progression: type a name, genre, scale or numerals (“metal”, “bVII”, “ii7 V7”) to narrow the New list.
  • Blank, improved: choose the key and any of 15 scales for the empty bars; every chordless bar shows “+ Chord” — click it to pick the chord, and its hits are added so it sounds at once. “+ Chord at cursor” in the Bar panel does the same.
  • Touch chord menu: long-press any cell, chord or bar number on a phone or tablet (iPhone and iPad included).
  • Settings, complete: Look & sound (theme, card style, strings, + Octave, test sound), Active cards, DAW song (summary, download, clear), Offline (status and Save everything now), Backup & reset (export / import every setting and the song, reset all). Changes reach an open Play page at once.
  • Installable offline app: icon, web-app manifest and theme colour on every page; a single shared offline file list; resilient caching.
  • Every page up to date: About, Reference, Release Notes and the Play header (now with Reference and Release Notes links) describe 1.0 as shipped.

What's new in 1.0

  • + Octave on every playable card. One switch, shared by all cards and remembered: chords and ascending lines gain the octave above their first note, descending lines finish an octave below.
  • Playback for every deck with pitches. Keys play their scale, Roman numerals their triad, strings their open note, Left Hand sequences one finger per fret, Right Hand paths their open strings in picking order, Base 0 cards their source card. Only Technique cards are silent.
  • Faster. Data tables build only when a deck needs them: the Play page starts in a few milliseconds, About appears instantly and fills its figures in afterwards, game redraws reuse their chooser lists.
  • Remembers where you were. The Play page reopens your last deck or game and string count; play.html#modes or play.html#game=… links open any deck or game directly, and every Reference entry has an "Open in Play →" link.
  • Fretboard Diagrams ready. The Fretboard link on every page header opens Fretboard Diagrams/index.html, the home of the companion app.
  • Release Notes (this page) on every header.
  • Every mode of the major scale in the Key deck. Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian and Locrian join Ionian (major) and Aeolian (natural minor) on all 12 tonics — 60 new cards with notes, formula, parent major key, signature and colour note, each spelled with the fewest accidentals.
  • Settings. Tick individual cards, whole groups, or entire decks to control which cards games may deal; switched-off cards remain browsable and appear dimmed on Play. Selections persist on this device, invalid saved data safely falls back, and storage failures are reported inline.
  • Contents on the Play page. "Choose deck" or "Choose game" opens a concise list of all 21 decks followed by all 131 games, grouped and linked.
  • Theory lessons. The Reference page opens with a full music-theory lesson for every deck: the idea, how it is built (with examples in C), hearing it, on the guitar, a practice plan, common mistakes and where it leads.
  • Reference sections collapse by default. Theory lessons, Decks, Games and DAW each open independently, with keyboard-accessible section controls.
  • From the Author. The "From the Creator" page is now "From the Author" (author.html).
  • Steady header. Every page shows every header link; the page you are on stays in place, marked, instead of disappearing. The Play page keeps a short header — Home, Play, DAW, Settings — so the cards and games get the room.
  • DAW. A new DAW page (daw.html, on every header) builds songs from cards: a grid of melody, chord and bass lanes down to 32nd notes; every bar with its own meter (1–32 over 2–32), beat grouping (2+2+3…), tempo, key, scale and chord changes; progression cards turned into sections (½–4 bars per chord, repeated, in any key); chord, Roman numeral, key, scale, pattern and interval cards set chords and scales or insert melody; sections arranged into a song; and live key, scale and chord changes while it plays. Songs save on the device and export / import as files.
  • DAW: blank or ready-made songs. New starts from empty bars for a totally new progression, or from 816 ready-made progressions (pop, rock, folk, jazz, blues, gospel, minor, harmonic minor, the modes, early music) in any key, meter, tempo and bars per chord.
  • DAW: chord menu and All bars. Right-click any cell, chord or bar number (or press C, or click a chord name) to add a triad, 4-tone or 5-tone chord at that point; the All bars strip changes chord, key, scale, meter and tempo of every bar in the section or song, and transposes by semitones.
  • DAW: 612 more built-in progressions. The local library now has 816 selections across 10 groups, with no download or service dependency.
  • DAW: export to JSON, TXT and CSV, triplet and dotted note values, keyboard editing of the grid, four sounds per lane, Redo.
  • Sound on iPhone and iPad with the silent switch on. The app now asks for the "playback" audio session (iOS 17+) and, on older iOS, keeps a silent local media stream running while sound is in use, so the hardware switch no longer mutes playback.

Use cases

  • Daily theory drill — open a deck, flip cards and name the notes before you look; play the card to check your ear.
  • Ear training — press ▶ before flipping and identify the interval, chord quality, scale or progression by sound; use + Octave to hear the frame of the line.
  • Practice routines — deal a game, play the subject in the dealt key with the dealt technique, then 🔀 one slot at a time to vary just one thing.
  • Teaching — pick cards manually with the chooser to build a lesson, and share direct links such as play.html#triads.
  • Improvisation prompts — Fibonacci progressions and note sets give endless, non-repeating material in any key and mode.
  • Technique building — Left Hand finger sequences and Right Hand string paths cover every combination for 2- to 8-string instruments.
  • Composition — combine a progression deck card with a melodic pattern and mode to sketch a part, then build it into a song in the DAW.
  • Song sketching — build a verse from one progression card and a chorus from another, arrange A A B A, and hear it with chords, bass and melody.
  • Backing tracks — loop a section in the DAW at any tempo and practise scales, patterns or improvisation over it; switch key, scale or chord live to drill changes.
  • Odd meters — set 7/8 as 2+2+3, 5/4, 12/8 or 9/16 with the click on and play along until the grouping feels natural.
  • Reharmonisation — keep the melody (stored as scale degrees) and change chords, key or scale bar by bar to hear what changes.
07

Applying it on the guitar

  • Intervals: find both notes on one string (the semitone count is the fret distance), then across adjacent strings.
  • Chords: play the root-position shape, then each inversion on the same string set; check the written bass note is lowest.
  • Scales and modes: play one octave in position, then on one string, then across the neck; + Octave closes the scale on its octave.
  • Melodic patterns: run each pattern through the dealt scale, ascending then descending, at a steady tempo.
  • Progressions: strum each chord for a bar while the app plays it; then arpeggiate with the dealt technique.
  • Left Hand: one finger per fret on the dealt string, in the written finger order; move up a fret and repeat.
  • Right Hand: pick the string path with open strings first, then fret a chord and keep the path.
  • Base 0: the formula is key-independent — play it from any root to move the idea anywhere on the neck.
  • DAW: loop a section as a backing track and play the chords, then the bass line, then the melody on guitar; use the live key buttons to practise the same part in every key.

Offline

  • Every runtime file is local. For the complete integrated package, serve the extracted folder on localhost without internet; direct file:// opening is not fully supported because modules, fetch and storage policies vary.
  • Served over HTTPS or localhost, both workers use the shared parent-app manifest. Installation must finish successfully before disconnecting. Cached files are served immediately, without waiting for a network timeout.
  • First-visit links wait at most 2.5 s for the cache, so the next page is available offline. A hosted first visit needs a connection to download the app once. A complete extracted package served on localhost needs no internet connection.
  • Every Fretboard Diagrams asset, the main fretboard, multi-diagram exports, Cadence Map and the testing page are included in the complete install.
  • One file list (offline-assets.js) drives both the service worker and Settings → Offline. Files are cached one by one, so a single failed download never leaves the app uncached; Settings shows how many files are saved and can save them all again.
  • Installable: every page links a web-app manifest and icon, so “Add to Home Screen” / “Install app” opens Theory Decks full-screen, offline, like a native app.
  • Offline folder addresses (Fretboard%20Diagrams/) open the folder's own page; a file never saved on the device shows a clear offline message instead of a browser error.

Known limitations

Every limitation from earlier notes, what 1.0 now does about it, and what is left (and why).

  • Data lives in this browser. Addressed: the app asks the browser to make its storage persistent, so it is never cleared automatically when the device runs low on space; Settings → Offline shows whether it is protected and how much is used, with a button to ask again. Backup & reset exports everything to one file, the whole-app .zip carries a backup too, and the DAW exports songs as JSON or MIDI (both import back). Left: clearing site data yourself still removes it — restore it from a backup.
  • A hosted first visit needs a connection. Addressed: Settings → Offline → Download the whole app (.zip) packs every file (from the offline copy, so it works with no connection) plus a backup of your settings and song; unzip it and open index.html on any computer, from any folder or USB stick, with no connection ever. Left: on a hosted site the files must reach a device once — after that, offline for good.
  • Background tabs. Addressed: the DAW's clock now runs in a small Web Worker built on the device, which browsers do not slow down in hidden tabs; playback keeps exact time and schedules only 0.4 s ahead while hidden (live changes are heard within 0.12 s once the tab is visible). Browsers without workers fall back to 1.5 s ahead.
  • Double flats in played-pitch lists. Fixed: chord, scale and pattern cards are spelled from their formula letter by letter — C°7 plays C4 E♭4 G♭4 B𝄫4, B+ plays B4 D♯5 F𝄪5, F♯ Lydian ends on E♯5 — including the added octave and dealt keys.
  • No drum lane; only JSON imported back. Addressed: a Drums lane (Mix → Drums: Beat, Half-time, Four on the floor, Hi-hat only) follows every bar's meter and beat groups, swings with the song, has its own volume and mute, and exports to MIDI channel 10. Import opens MIDI: files exported here reopen exactly (the song travels inside the .mid), and any other MIDI file brings in its tempo, meter, key and melody line. MIDI key signatures now come with a text event naming the exact key and scale (“Key: E Phrygian dominant”). Left: sounds are synthesised on the device by design (no audio files keeps the app small and fully offline); TXT and CSV are reading and spreadsheet formats; chords are not guessed from other programs' MIDI files.
  • iOS silent-switch stream. Fixed: the silent media stream now runs only while sound plays — it pauses 4 s after the last note and at once when the page is hidden or closed.
  • Very low strings on small speakers. Addressed: notes below about G2 (7- and 8-string B1, F♯1, low bass notes) get stronger 2nd–4th harmonics and a loudness lift of up to ×1.9, so their pitch is clearly heard even where the speaker cannot reproduce the fundamental; the pitch stays exact. Left: a phone speaker physically cannot play 46 Hz — headphones or larger speakers give the true low end.
